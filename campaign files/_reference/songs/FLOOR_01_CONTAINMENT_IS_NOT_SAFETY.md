# FLOOR I — Containment Is Not Safety

Status: Eleven Music v2 six-minute exploration-ambience draft, lexically audited 2026-07-18.

## Narrative function

- Location: Tower Floor 1.
- Musical role: general exploration ambience, not a boss theme.
- Tutorial lesson: danger.
- Reveal level: the floor sounds protected by ancient machinery, but protection is an active process repeatedly resisting Chaos.
- Diegetic vocal source: an ancient containment system using a synthetic Reyvateil-like voice as an interface.
- Execution status: the supplied Hymmnos packets are executable Standard Hymmnos. All other vocal texture must be non-lexical vowels or breath.
- Dramatic thesis: containment is not safety. Silence can hold a wound.

## Eleven Music v2 prompt

```text
Create a six-minute dark science-fantasy dungeon ambience titled “Floor I — Containment Is Not Safety.” This is exploration music for the first tutorial floor of an ancient Tower: survivable because old technology constantly holds Chaos back, but never truly safe. The floor’s lesson is danger.

Use 68 BPM, restrained 4/4, and a cold E Phrygian tonal center. The technological layer is coherent and exact: glassy FM bells, pure sine tones, quiet relay clicks, low electrical drones, distant metal resonance, a soft two-note machine pulse, and clean phase-locked harmonics. It should feel ancient, sophisticated, and still operational—not modern cyberpunk and not a spaceship engine room.

Use a sparse synthetic female-coded virtual-singer lead with bright artificial formants, hyper-clear syllables, impossible breath control, and subtle layered harmonies. She appears only in three short containment packets, never as a continuous pop vocalist. Sing exactly the supplied Hymmnos and generate no additional words. During corrupted passages, voices may glitch into chopped non-lexical vowels and breath only. Keep the vocal sound eerie, beautiful, artificial, and slightly inhuman.

The music must be imperfect and frightening. Add clipped reverb tails, isolated buffer stutters, pitch drift, phase cancellation, momentary dropouts, reversed vocal grains, microtonal beating, and brief discordant clusters. Do not keep a pristine cinematic mix. Chaos should assault the composition in four different ways, and the ancient system should forcibly re-synchronize it each time:

0:00–0:55 — ANCIENT COHERENCE. Sparse technological ambience, stable pulse, wide empty space. A faint synthetic voice performs the first maintenance line once. No obvious melody and no percussion groove.

0:55–1:20 — BREACH I: TUNING. One stable note bends downward, nearby tones develop microtonal beating, whispers appear in the wrong stereo positions, and the sub-bass swells like pressure behind a wall. No jump-scare volume spike. At 1:20, three hard synchronization clicks force every tone back onto the grid.

1:20–2:20 — CONTAINMENT I. The machine pulse returns with a hairline crack: one reverb tail cuts off too early. The singer delivers the two-line warning packet around 1:35, then disappears. Leave long instrumental space afterward.

2:20–2:52 — BREACH II: RHYTHM. The pulse skips, repeats partial beats, and briefly implies incompatible meters. Use chopped non-lexical synthetic vowels as corrupted data, not lyrics. At 2:52, a low mechanical chord quantizes the rhythm back into 4/4.

2:52–3:45 — CONTAINMENT II. Add restrained low frame-drum impacts far in the background and a narrow-band machine choir. The singer delivers the two-line defense packet once. The restored music should be controlled, not comforting.

3:45–4:25 — BREACH III: IDENTITY AND SPACE. This is the longest and eeriest assault. Reverse and granulate memories of the synthetic voice without forming words. Let sounds rotate around the listener, vanish in phase cancellation, and return as detuned copies. Introduce an irregular low-frequency pressure wave and dissonant bowed-metal clusters. The technology nearly loses the tonal center.

4:25–5:10 — CONTAINMENT III. A descending diagnostic tone scans the mix. The original machine lattice returns, but one note is permanently missing. The singer delivers the final two-line warning: calm does not mean harmless. Keep several seconds of near-silence after her.

5:10–5:40 — BREACH IV: COMBINED AFTERSHOCK. A shorter, denser attack combining pitch drift, skipped timing, corrupted vowel grains, and one abrupt channel dropout. It should feel dangerous but remain usable under tabletop dialogue; avoid extreme loudness.

5:40–6:00 — FORCED STABILITY. The ancient system locks the floor down with clean relay clicks and the opening two-note pulse. Return close to the opening texture so the track can loop or crossfade into an instrumental version, but leave one quiet detuned tone under the final chord to prove Chaos was contained rather than destroyed. End with a long controlled decay, not a triumphant resolution.

Overall: eerie as hell, tense, corrupted, spacious, technologically coherent only through constant effort. No heroic melody, no lush fantasy orchestra, no conventional verse/chorus, no EDM drop, no trailer braams, no rock drums, no catchy ostinato repeated for six minutes, no bright resolution, no random language, and no continuous singing.
```

## Literal and poetic translation

| Hymmnos | Conservative literal parse | Floor-level meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `Ma ki ra hymme guard roon.` | In stable focus that I want continued, I sing/resonate protection through the room. | The ancient system is actively maintaining the floor. |
| `Was paks ga knawa rre dazua enter roon.` | In intense nervousness that I want to end, I know that darkness enters the room. | A breach is detected. `Darkness` is the contextual image for Chaos, not a dictionary translation of Chaos. |
| `Was granme ra knawa rre guard giue dazua.` | In intense protectiveness that I want continued, I know that protection imprisons darkness. | Ancient technology contains the incursion. |
| `Was guwo ga knawa rre dazua zethpa guard.` | In intense anger that I want to end, I know that darkness cuts/slashes protection. | The next attack damages the containment field. |
| `Was granme ra khal roon.` | In intense protectiveness that I want continued, I protect the room. | The system reasserts its function. |
| `Ma ki wa knawa rre wirtamarls haf xava.` | In stable focus that I accept as it is, I know that silence holds a wound. | The tutorial lesson: apparent calm can contain danger. |

The closing repetition of `Ma ki ra hymme guard roon` does not claim that the floor is safe. It confirms only that protection is running again.

The ancient system/interface is the speaker and owns every Emotion Sound. In each `knawa rre ...` construction, `rre` marks the following noun as an external subject.

## Future instrumental companion

For an instrumental version, reuse the complete music prompt with these substitutions:

```text
Instrumental only. Remove all sung words and vocal lead. Preserve the three containment moments by assigning the exact syllabic rhythm and melodic contour of each missing Hymmnos packet to a glass harmonica layered with a narrow formant synth. Non-lexical corrupted vocal grains may remain during breaches, but no intelligible words may appear.
```

Keep both generations at 68 BPM, six minutes, and the same timeline. The safest crossfade points for knotting the versions together are the forced re-locks at approximately 1:20, 2:52, 4:25, and 5:40. Crossfade during coherent plateaus, not during breaches, so the change of source feels like part of the Tower's resynchronization.

## Listening checks

1. Vocals occupy less than roughly forty seconds of the six-minute cue.
2. No extra English or pseudo-Hymmnos words are generated.
3. Each breach damages a different musical rule: tuning, rhythm, identity/space, then a combined aftershock.
4. Restorations sound mechanical and forced, not warmly resolved.
5. Loudness remains usable beneath table conversation; danger comes from instability rather than jump-scare volume.
6. The 5:40 texture is close enough to the opening for future looping or crossfading.
7. One audible imperfection survives the ending.
