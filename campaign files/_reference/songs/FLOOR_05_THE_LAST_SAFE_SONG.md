# FLOOR V — The Last Safe Song

Status: Eleven Music v2 six-minute exploration-ambience draft, lexically audited 2026-07-18.

## Narrative function

- This floor teaches no discrete rule. It ends the protected tutorial and releases the party into the real Tower.
- Emotional image: a parent dropping a fledgling so it must discover flight while falling.
- Musical role: sad farewell, doomed good-luck charm, recapitulation of Floors 1–4, and the final frontier of ancient protection.
- Diegetic singer: the ancient protective system expressed through one Reyvateil-like voice.
- Thesis: the system cannot make the party safe. Its final act of care is to stop catching them.

## Generation method

Use one six-minute generation. Paste the music prompt into Eleven Music's prompt field and the plain eleven-line block into its custom lyric field. Do not add headings, translations, or performance directions to the lyric field.

The vocal remains one continuous passage by one singer. Glitches may damage timing, timbre, accompaniment, and the spaces between words, but they must never replace, repeat, or obscure the supplied text.

## Validation notes

- Every lyric token occurs exactly in the campaign lexicon.
- All words are Central Standard Note or dialect-unmarked.
- Every line uses a complete three-part Emotion Sound.
- `rre` and subject-form `yorr` correctly mark external subjects.
- `na` is the attested Central negation and directly precedes the negated subject verbs `swant` and `toya`.
- “The charm fails” is not claimed as a nonexistent direct translation: good fortune not saving the listener and protection not following beyond the boundary create that contextual meaning.
- The passage deliberately ends with belief rather than knowledge. The system cannot certify flight, but chooses to release and believe.
